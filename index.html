About

I am an Industrial PhD student at BrainCapture and the Section for Digital Health at DTU Health Tech, where I lead the development of large EEG foundation models.

My research uses self-supervised learning to learn general-purpose representations of brain activity, and mechanistic interpretability to open those models up: turning what they have learned into features clinicians can inspect and trust. Working between a university and a medtech company, I care about results that survive the move from benchmark to clinic, including on low-cost hardware in resource-limited settings.

BrainCapture

Industry

Bringing EEG to where it is missing

BrainCapture is a Danish medtech company building a low-cost, portable EEG system: an electrode cap and a smartphone-based recorder that let health workers without specialist training record clinical-grade EEG, with analysis supported remotely and by machine learning.

I joined as a data scientist in 2021 and now lead its foundation-model research as an Industrial PhD, connecting the machine learning to the product and to clinical studies in the field.
